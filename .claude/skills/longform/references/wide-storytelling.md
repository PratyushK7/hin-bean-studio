# Wide-format documentary storytelling

Use with `.claude/skills/longform/SKILL.md`.

## Wide frame

- Compose for 16:9 landscape. Confirm the renderer's world and output dimensions from its configuration; do not hard-code assumptions from another version of the engine.
- A wide frame supports side-by-side comparison, map + annotation, process diagrams and data panels. Use this room deliberately.
- Keep primary labels and data away from platform UI overlays and the bottom-right duration badge in thumbnails.
- Design every key visual so it remains legible at normal desktop viewing and mobile feed scale.

## Documentary staging

Use visual hierarchy:
1. Main subject or data relationship.
2. Essential label and units.
3. Secondary evidence or source context.
4. Optional atmosphere.

Use a map when geography is central, a timeline when sequence matters, a flow diagram when causality matters, and a chart only when the data supports a quantitative comparison. Do not make every chapter a full-screen text card.

## Scene variation

Change the visual form when the explanatory question changes. Reuse components, not entire scenes. A single investigation may alternate between:
- a full map;
- a timeline with source documents;
- a labelled process diagram;
- a chart derived from an actual dataset;
- a close-up of a physical object;
- a quiet summary frame.

## Data graphics

- Store source values and units in a machine-readable file.
- State whether the scale is linear, logarithmic, indexed or normalised.
- Start chart axes at a meaningful baseline; when not starting at zero, show and explain that choice.
- Do not animate a chart in a way that exaggerates a small difference.
- Show source and period in a compact, readable caption.

## Music and sound

- Use a restrained underscore. The narration is the primary sound.
- Change the music mood at major narrative turns only when the renderer supports reliable mood control.
- Use sound effects for meaningful physical events or transitions, not as constant punctuation.
- When a fact or conclusion needs emphasis, try a deliberate quiet beat before adding music or impact sounds.

## Chapters and rhythm

- Chapter timestamps must come from actual voice timing.
- A chapter starts when its argument starts, not just when the visual changes.
- A viewer who skips to a chapter should understand what question the section answers.
- End each chapter with a genuine takeaway or a reason to continue.
