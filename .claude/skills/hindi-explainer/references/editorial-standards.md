# Editorial standards

These standards apply to every script, storyboard, video, thumbnail and community-facing description.

## Accuracy

- Every material factual statement must map to one or more source IDs in the claim ledger.
- Attribute estimates and uncertain claims in the narration itself when the distinction matters.
- Do not convert "may", "is associated with" or "in some studies" into "does" or "causes".
- Never invent an expert quote, institutional position, statistic, date, image caption or study result.
- Explain the limits of a dataset or study when those limits could change the viewer's interpretation.
- For competing explanations, represent the strength of the evidence rather than manufacturing symmetry.

## Editorial independence

- The title and thumbnail must accurately represent the video.
- No fabricated urgency, fake controversy, conspiracy framing or outrage bait.
- Do not conceal a key caveat to make a hook stronger.
- Do not present an opinion as a settled fact.
- Separate reported fact, analysis and the narrator's interpretation.

## Hindi quality

- Narration is written in Devanagari and sounds natural aloud.
- Hindi should be contemporary and accessible, not artificially formal.
- Keep standard technical English terms when they improve understanding; explain specialist vocabulary.
- Use consistent spellings for people, places, institutions and technical terms.
- Numeric and date conventions must be consistent across narration, visuals and subtitles.
- On-screen Hindi labels must use a font with complete Devanagari glyph coverage. Verify conjuncts, matras and line breaks in rendered frames.

## Visual evidence

- A chart must identify its source, measure, unit and time range.
- A map must not imply territorial or political claims beyond the sourced boundary data.
- Archival imagery must be labelled or contextualised accurately.
- Reconstructed scenes must not be confused with documentary footage.
- A visual analogy must be clearly metaphorical when a literal reading would mislead.
- Do not use a decorative graph when the data is too weak to support it.

## Production quality

- Narration must be intelligible on phone speakers.
- Hindi subtitles must preserve the spoken wording and punctuation, not be mechanically translated.
- Keep music below the voice and remove music when silence makes the point stronger.
- No unreviewed placeholder text, broken glyphs, overlapping labels, watermarks or source errors.
- Every final video receives a full audio pass and a frame/contact-sheet review before upload.

## Corrections

When a factual error is found after publication, record the correction, update the source ledger and assess whether the video, description, pinned comment or a follow-up needs an explicit correction. Do not quietly rewrite the source notes and pretend the published version was always correct.
